DiplomacyJobs Design System

An audit of the public site as it stands on claude/great-cerf-a494ae, a token-based design system that keeps today's institutional look, and redesigned job list, job page and sources screens. The aim is to make the site calmer on phones and put the deadline first. Every claim it makes should stay visibly backed by evidence.

1 · What the site does today

The foundation is strong. The site has honest coverage states, quoted evidence for every extracted requirement, accessible labels, a bilingual interface and no JavaScript dependency. The issues below are mostly about hierarchy and density, plus three real bugs. Screens were captured from a local run with 8 sample jobs and the real 87-organization registry.

2 · Design principles

Four rules to settle future design arguments. They come from what the product already promises in PLAN.md: official sources, honest coverage and Bosnian first.

01

Evidence beside every claim

Every extracted fact can show the sentence it came from, and every count shows when it was checked. Unknown stays unknown: "—", never 0. The design gives evidence a consistent, quiet form (a quote block with a brand rule) so it's always one tap away and never clutter.

02

Deadline first

A job seeker's first question is "can I still apply?" The deadline is the second-strongest element on a card, after the title, and the only one with urgency colour. Nothing else on the card uses red or amber.

03

Calm on a phone

Most visitors in BiH will arrive on mobile from a shared link. The first screen shows results. Secondary controls go behind one disclosure, and the apply action stays within thumb reach.

04

One meaning per colour

Blue means the brand and links. Green means a source is covered, red means it ends today, amber means it ends soon or there is a warning, violet means in review, and grey means not tracked. Colour always comes with a text label.

4 · Components

Each component maps to an existing template partial so it can land without restructuring views. Class names in the mockups use a ui- prefix only to keep them apart from this page's styles. In site.css they would replace the current names one-to-one (.job-card, .status-pill, …).

Job card v2

Anatomy
  1. Employer monogram, from the organization's short name, with an initials fallback. Names of 5 or more letters use the 9.5px --long size. Hidden on mobile.
  2. Employer line in 14px regular. "putem ReliefWeb" follows in muted text, and the Novo pill sits here.
  3. Title (h3), a stretched link to the job page. A translated title goes underneath.
  4. Meta row with SVG icons: city, type, scope.
  5. Up to 3 tags.
  6. Eligibility as one amber line with an icon, not a filled block.
  7. Side column: deadline on top, with the secondary external link below it.
Behaviour and a11y
  • The whole card is clickable. The external button sits above the stretched link (z-index:1) and keeps its own target.
  • Hover raises the border to --color-border-strong and adds --shadow-2, with no movement.
  • Under 640px the side column becomes a footer row: deadline left, button right.
  • "Detalji i uslovi" is removed, since the title link covers it, and the external button keeps its visually hidden job title.
  • The "Provjereno" date moves to the job page. The trust strip says "checked today" once for the whole list.

Search and filter bar

Structure
  • Primary row: search (2.2fr), City, Type, Filters (n) and Search. These are the three filters people use most, and Hick's law favours fewer choices up front.
  • Filters is a <details> that holds organization, scope, education, experience and field. It opens by default when any of them is set. Sort moves to the results header.
  • Applied chips are plain <a> elements built from page_query minus one key, so they need no JavaScript.
Rules
  • Placeholder text says what the search covers. The query persists in the field.
  • The badge on Filters counts only hidden active filters.
  • Zero results: repeat the query, offer to remove the last filter, and link to /sources/ (the existing copy stays).
  • On mobile the search field takes the full row, with City and Type side by side underneath and the two buttons below them.

6 · Rollout

The work is ordered so the launch blockers come first and each step can be shipped and reviewed on its own. Django view logic doesn't change. The work is in CSS and templates, plus one small template tag.

Phase 1

Done 3 Oct 2026

  1. Fix mobile overflow: a two-row header under 760px, with the language switch moved out of the nav (base.html, site.css). No page scrolls sideways at 320, 360 or 390px.
  2. Adopt tokens: board/static/board/tokens.css is linked before site.css, and site.css has no raw hex values left. Dark mode is defined but opt-in (<html data-theme="auto">), so it stays off until QA.
  3. Accessibility: add a global :focus-visible ring, set --color-border-control on inputs and enlarge the language switch targets.
  4. Status colours: review is violet. Covered-with-zero jobs is green, like covered. Awaiting integration, unchecked and not found share grey and are told apart by their labels.
  5. Icons: 📍, ↗ and ◎ are replaced by an inline SVG sprite (templates/board/_icons.html).

Phase 2

Launch week · 2–3 days

  1. Filter bar: a primary row plus a <details> for requirement filters, and applied chips built from page_query (jobs.html, a small helper in views.py).
  2. Job card v2 with a {% deadline job %} inclusion tag (board_extras.py) that the card, the job page and the sticky bar share.
  3. Job page: a summary grid under the title, a sticky apply bar under 960px and the restyled evidence quotes (job_detail.html).
  4. Trust strip on the job list. Reuse the coverage totals from views.sources as a cached helper.
  5. Self-host Public Sans (two woff2 weights) under board/static/board/fonts/.

Phase 3

After launch

  1. Sources page: stat cards as filters, grouped rows, a coverage bar and name search.
  2. Dark theme: it is already defined in the tokens, so only a QA pass on the real templates is needed.
  3. Employer monograms. Add an optional short_name to Organization (e.g. "OSCE", "UNFPA").
  4. An Open Graph share image per job (title, employer and deadline on a brand background), for links shared in Viber and WhatsApp groups.
  5. Run a usability check with 5 job seekers on mobile using the tasks "find a job you qualify for in Sarajevo" and "apply before the deadline".
